The problem

2.4Mpeople

India is home to about 2.4 million deaf and speech-impaired people, around a fifth of the world's deaf population.

Most people never learned sign language.

Sign language users struggle to talk with people who do not sign. That leaves them isolated and makes everyday services, from a clinic visit to a shop counter, harder to use.

Our glove sits between the two sides. The signer moves their hand as usual, and the other person reads the words.

What already exists

Motion-sensor gloves

Commercial gloves convert signs to text and speech with motion sensors, aimed at independence for deaf and hard-of-hearing users worldwide.

Flex sensors and deep learning

Research gloves classify hand movements in real time and read them aloud, with accurate gesture recognition.

Camera and Raspberry Pi

Image-processing devices recognise a fixed set of gestures and show them as text, but depend on a camera view of the hand.

What we set out to do

Make communication easy

Sign in, text out, with no interpreter in the room.

Keep it user friendly

Connect over Bluetooth, pick a language, start signing.

Keep it light

A glove people can wear all day without noticing the electronics.

The solution

One glove, two modes.

The same wearable serves deaf and speech-impaired users and bedbound patients who need continuous care.

Mode 1

Gesture Sync

  • Real-time sign language to text.
  • Sensor-equipped glove, so no camera is needed.
  • Connects signers and non-signers in the same conversation.
  • Gives deaf users a way to communicate on their own.
Flex sensor→Microcontroller→Website→Gesture recognition→Text prediction

Mode 2

Continuous Care

  • Health sensors watch the patient all the time.
  • Readings appear on a website anyone with access can open from anywhere.
  • Every reading is logged to Google Sheets with a date and time.
  • Alerts can go out when something looks wrong.
Health sensors→Microcontroller→Firebase→Website / Google Sheets
Who it is for
